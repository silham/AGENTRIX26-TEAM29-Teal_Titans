========================================================================
SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM  (0:20 – 1:05)
========================================================================

SCRIPT
  "Here's the thing — this information is already public. Every
  department has a website.

  So try this. You've lost your ID card, and you need a passport.

  [PAUSE]

  Nowhere online will tell you that your passport application is blocked
  until you get a duplicate NIC. That the duplicate NIC needs a police
  report. That the police report needs your birth certificate. Nobody
  tells you the order.

  So you find out at the counter. You go home. You come back. You bring
  the wrong document, or you pay a broker to know it for you.

  The information isn't missing. The sequence is."

SPEAKER NOTES
  · This is your strongest 45 seconds. Slow down here, don't rush it.
  · The three cards on screen — wrong order, scattered rules, repeat
    visits — should never be read aloud. The story covers all three.
  · "The information isn't missing. The sequence is." → land it, pause,
    then click through. That sentence is what a judge repeats later.
  · If a judge is Sri Lankan, this lands as lived experience. Watch for
    the nod and don't over-explain past it.


========================================================================
SLIDE 3 — THE APP  (1:05 – 1:50)
========================================================================

SCRIPT
  "So we built the sequence.

  HelpLK AI is not a chatbot. It's a stateful case manager. Eight
  specialist agents — a Planner, a Knowledge agent, a Dependency agent,
  an Eligibility agent and so on — running on a LangGraph state machine.

  Two things make it safe enough for government.

  First, it's hybrid. What's locked, and who's eligible, is decided by
  deterministic rules — never by the model. The model only writes the
  explanation, and every step is cited back to an official document.

  Second, it remembers. Government procedures take weeks. You can pause
  today, come back in a month, and the case is exactly where you left
  it — in Sinhala, Tamil or English."

SPEAKER NOTES
  · The four cards map to the four beats: Agentic, Grounded, Stateful,
    Trilingual. Let the slide do that work silently.
  · "Never by the model" is your anti-hallucination answer, delivered
    before anyone has to ask it. Say it deliberately.
  · Don't list all eight agents. Three plus "and so on" sounds like a
    system; eight sounds like a slide.

SLIDE 4 — HOW IT WORKS  (1:50 – 2:30)
========================================================================

SCRIPT
  "Four steps, and I'll use the same case.

  The citizen types 'I lost my NIC and I need a passport.' Plain
  language, any of the three languages.

  The Planner spots two services. The Dependency agent locks the
  passport step and tells you exactly why it's locked. The Checklist
  agent turns that into your documents.

  You upload what you have — the Document agent reads it and tells you
  what's missing or unsigned.

  And it tracks. Progress, next action, what's still blocked.

  Four steps. Describe, plan, prepare, track."

SPEAKER NOTES
  · Gesture along the numbered track 1-2-3-4 as you go. It syncs the
    room to the slide without you narrating the labels.
  · "Tells you exactly why it's locked" — this is explainability, and
    it is the feature government buyers actually care about.
  · Close on the four verbs so the slide resolves cleanly into the demo.

========================================================================
Q&A — ANSWER IN ONE BREATH, THEN STOP
========================================================================

"Why not just ChatGPT?"
  "ChatGPT is stateless and ungrounded. We add case memory, dependency
  rules, document verification and an audit trail. The LLM is one
  component inside a governed system — it isn't the system."

"How do you know it won't hallucinate a requirement?"
  "Locking and eligibility are deterministic rules from procedure JSON,
  never the model. The model writes explanation only, and every step
  carries a citation the citizen can click."

"Is the Sinhala and Tamil real, or translated captions?"
  "Real. Input is normalised to English on the way in, output is
  translated at the API boundary. The graph, rules and database stay
  English-only — so switching language is a pure read, zero writes,
  and nothing in the citizen's case can be lost by it."

"How current is the government information?"
  "Sourced from official pages and PDFs, indexed with a citation on
  every answer. Staff re-index through the admin page — you saw it in
  the demo. And when confidence is low we say so rather than guess."

"What's the business model?"
  "Government or its digital-transformation partner. The value is
  measurable on their side: incomplete applications, repeat visits,
  help-desk volume."

"What if a procedure changes tomorrow?"
  "That's why rules and content are data, not code. Update the
  procedure JSON or re-index the document — no deployment."
